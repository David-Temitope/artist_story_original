# Artist Story - The Living Consequence Engine

This is one of the most important systems in Artist Story.
Every agent working on this codebase must strictly adhere to these core principles to ensure the simulation remains dynamic, emergent, and system-driven.

## Core Philosophy

The game must never become a puzzle with one correct solution.
There should never be a universal strategy that always works.
The same decision made under different circumstances should produce different outcomes because the surrounding conditions have changed.

Players should never say:
> "I know exactly what will happen if I click this."

Instead they should think:
> "Based on everything happening in my career, I think this is the best decision."

That is how real life works.

---

## Context Over Rules

Every important decision should be evaluated within its current context.
Context includes, but is not limited to:
- Current economy
- Public opinion
- Relationships
- Industry trends
- Market confidence
- Genre popularity
- World events
- Player reputation
- Player integrity
- Player experience
- Past decisions
- Current stress
- Business strength
- Available cash
- Timing
- NPC personalities
- Regional culture
- Media attention
- Luck (small influence only)

The outcome of a decision should emerge from the interaction of these systems rather than a fixed rule.

---

## Decisions Create Probability, Not Destiny

A good decision should improve the probability of positive outcomes.
It should never guarantee success.
A poor decision should increase the probability of negative consequences.
It should never guarantee failure.

Examples:
- Hiring an excellent manager increases the chance of securing better sponsorships. It does not guarantee sponsorships.
- Writing an outstanding song increases the chance of commercial success. It does not guarantee a hit.
- Maintaining integrity increases trust from brands and partners. It does not guarantee every company will work with the player.

This keeps the world believable while ensuring player choices matter.

---

## Wisdom Must Compound

Good decisions should not simply produce immediate rewards.
They should improve the player's ability to make better decisions in the future.

Examples:
- Learning financial discipline improves budgeting, allowing players to recognize stronger investment opportunities and avoid unnecessary risks.
- Building trustworthy relationships expands professional networks, increasing the likelihood of hearing about valuable opportunities before others.
- Consistently honoring contracts improves reputation, causing higher-quality partners to become willing to negotiate.
- Developing songwriting skills helps players recognize stronger creative ideas and collaborate more effectively.

The player should feel that experience creates better judgment.
Knowledge should become one of the most valuable assets in the game.

---

## Experience Should Reduce Mistakes

Players who repeatedly practice good habits should gradually make fewer costly mistakes.

Examples:
- Experienced investors identify warning signs earlier.
- Experienced managers negotiate stronger contracts.
- Experienced tour managers plan tours more efficiently.
- Experienced business managers recognize unsustainable growth before it becomes dangerous.

Experience should unlock better options rather than automatic success.

---

## Good Decisions Are Long-Term Investments

The game should reward consistent good judgment over time.

Examples:
- Being honest with business partners may reduce short-term profit but increases long-term trust.
- Treating employees well improves loyalty, reducing turnover and improving company performance.
- Helping another artist early in the game may eventually create opportunities years later.
- Living below one's means creates financial flexibility during economic downturns.

These rewards should emerge naturally from the simulation.

---

## Every Success Creates New Challenges

Success should introduce complexity rather than ending the game.

Examples:
- Growing popularity attracts more media scrutiny.
- Higher income creates higher taxes and operating costs.
- Owning more businesses increases management complexity.
- Hiring more employees increases payroll and leadership responsibilities.
- Large fan bases create greater expectations.

Success should evolve gameplay rather than simplify it.

---

## Every Failure Creates New Opportunities

Failure should not end progress.
Failure should change the path forward.

Examples:
- A failed album may push the player toward songwriting for others.
- A cancelled tour may create time to build a business.
- A rejected record deal may inspire the player to become independent.
- A market crash may create investment opportunities.

The game should always offer meaningful ways to recover.

---

## Human Behavior Is Never Perfectly Predictable

NPCs should not follow scripts.
Every NPC has evolving personalities, goals, experiences, fears, ambitions, relationships, and changing opinions.
- A person who refused to collaborate last year may now be interested because circumstances have changed.
- A loyal employee may eventually resign after repeated burnout.
- A rival may unexpectedly become an ally against a common competitor.

The world should feel alive because people grow and change.

---

## The World Learns

The simulation should remember history.
- Brands remember professionalism.
- Fans remember authenticity.
- Employees remember leadership.
- Friends remember generosity.
- Journalists remember interviews.
- Record labels remember negotiations.
- Communities remember actions.

The player's reputation is built through accumulated behavior rather than isolated moments.

---

## Timing Matters

A brilliant decision at the wrong time may underperform.
An average decision at the perfect time may exceed expectations.

Examples:
- Launching an album during a global sporting event may reduce attention.
- Opening a luxury business during a recession may struggle.
- Investing during market panic may produce long-term gains.

Timing should become one of the most valuable strategic skills.

---

## Teach Through Consequences, Not Instructions

Never lecture the player.
Never force moral lessons.
Allow the simulation to demonstrate outcomes naturally.
Players should discover principles through experience.

If a player repeatedly practices discipline, patience, learning, generosity, wise stewardship, relationship building, and sound financial management, those habits should gradually increase the quality of future decisions—not because the game is giving hidden rewards, but because those habits naturally create better opportunities, stronger judgment, and more resilient careers.

Likewise, reckless, dishonest, impulsive, or short-sighted behavior should gradually narrow future options and increase risk, while still leaving room for redemption, recovery, and growth.

---

## Adaptability Is More Valuable Than Memorization

Players should never succeed by memorizing optimal choices.
They should succeed by understanding systems.
Every playthrough should require observation, learning, adaptation, and critical thinking.
The simulation should continually evolve so that strategies must evolve with it.
A strategy that was excellent five years ago may become outdated because the world has changed.
Players who understand why something worked will outperform players who only remember that it worked.

---

## Final Development Rule

Every new mechanic added to Artist Story must answer these questions:
1. What other systems influence this mechanic?
2. What systems does this mechanic influence?
3. How can this mechanic produce multiple believable outcomes?
4. What can the player learn from interacting with this mechanic?
5. How does this mechanic make future decisions more informed rather than more predictable?

If a mechanic has only one obvious outcome, redesign it.
If a mechanic has no long-term consequences, redesign it.
If a mechanic can be mastered through memorization instead of understanding, redesign it.
